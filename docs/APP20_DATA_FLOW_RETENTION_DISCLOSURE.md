# APP20 engineering data-flow and retention disclosure

> **Engineering disclosure only — not a privacy policy, terms, legal notice, or launch acceptance.** This document describes behavior evidenced by the cited repository code. It does not identify a controller, choose a lawful basis, promise a retention period, establish a rights process, or approve any deployment. The application has no privacy/terms route ([`src/app/routes.ts:1-15`](../src/app/routes.ts#L1-L15)). **Legal/data-protection acceptance remains open** pending an accountable operator, deployment-specific facts, and legal/regulatory/data-protection review; this document does not close that release gate ([`APP20_RELEASE_GATES.md`](APP20_RELEASE_GATES.md#gate-summary)).

## Scope and reading rules

This is a code-derived map for engineers and reviewers. Mail/escrow/RFQ execution descriptions below concern build-gated localnet behavior or dormant production seams; no live APP20 helper or public-network RFQ is configured, and the repository contains no Cloudflare deployment evidence. “Stored” means code deliberately writes a record; it does not imply that browser, platform, network, backup, observability, or upstream operators retain nothing else. “Expiry” means a lifecycle status or access window changes; it is **not deletion** unless the cited code removes the record. Browser deletion cannot delete copies already shared with recipients, services, backups, or a public chain.

## Public-chain facts and deletion limits

Encryption hides selected content; it does not make chain activity private or erasable. On-chain Mail ciphertext, size, timing, helper activity, and public mailbox-key registration remain public. Multi-recipient Mail leaks recipient count even though its tags are designed not to identify recipients. Shield/unshield amount and timing remain correlatable. In RFQ v3, `LockCreated` publishes lock/RFQ ids, pair, expiry, maximum B, the complete one-to-four-point schedule, and ticket; `takerCommitment` publishes the taker's ephemeral Stark public key; Take calldata publishes its signature and ordered fills; `LockTaken` publishes exact per-lock A/B and remaining B; `DealTaken` publishes exact aggregate A/B, fill count, and ordered `fillsDigest`; output-note ownership is not signature-bound, leaving a signed-Take copy-sniping race; maker settlement events publish output token/amount ([`escrow.cairo`](../cairo/src/escrow.cairo)). OPEN-note deposits and all public RPC submissions remain visible to providers and Starknet. An encrypted backup pointer hides its CID from chain plaintext, but the IPFS service receiving that CID and encrypted blob has the separate metadata boundary above.

Accordingly:

- deleting browser `localStorage`, `sessionStorage`, or IndexedDB does not delete chain ciphertext, registration, transactions, events, amounts, timing, or identifiers;
- expiry, cancellation, refusal, settlement, refund, locking a mailbox, and clearing a response cache are not global deletion;
- deleting a local contact does not retract an encrypted backup already posted or unpin a blob;
- clearing a sender’s Sent copy does not delete a recipient’s copy;
- RFQ deletion in one browser does not delete maker, transcript, coordinator, relay, IPFS, RPC, chain, log, backup, or counterparty copies; and
- no code can guarantee erasure from browser sync, device/OS backups, platform logs, RPC archives, indexers, explorers, or third-party backups.

## Implemented retention controls versus absent policy

### Implemented in code

- Explicit mailbox-profile purge for enumerated local mailbox prefixes and tab pending-payment handoff ([`local-mailbox-storage.ts:11-43`](../src/lib/local-mailbox-storage.ts#L11-L43), [`inbox/page.tsx:2200-2233`](../src/app/inbox/page.tsx#L2200-L2233)).
- Per-contact removal by encrypted-book rewrite ([`address-book.ts:279-289`](../src/lib/address-book.ts#L279-L289)).
- Pending payment handoff clear/consume ([`pending-payment.ts:39-58`](../src/lib/pending-payment.ts#L39-L58)).
- Restricted RFQ “forget” after selected terminal/local-non-authoritative states, with fail-closed tombstoning ([`rfq-lifecycle.ts`](../src/app/rfq/rfq-lifecycle.ts), [`rfq-storage.ts`](../src/app/rfq/rfq-storage.ts)).
- Terminal v3 rows remove the ephemeral `takerSigningKey`; backup export recursively strips signing-key fields. The public key, signed ordered fills, and chain events remain public ([`rfq-lifecycle.ts`](../src/app/rfq/rfq-lifecycle.ts), [`rfq-history-backup.ts`](../src/lib/rfq-history-backup.ts)).
- Snapshot scope/digest/MAC verification; authenticated-before-rank candidate selection; durable sequence high-water; bounded portable RFQ tombstones; permanent verify-only `restoredFromBackup` provenance; AES-GCM blob authentication; bounded 4,096-byte buckets; upload CID/size verification; and multi-gateway CID re-verification ([`backup-snapshot.ts`](../src/lib/backup-snapshot.ts), [`rfq-history-backup.ts`](../src/lib/rfq-history-backup.ts), [`backup-blob.ts`](../src/lib/backup-blob.ts), [`blob-store.ts`](../src/lib/blob-store.ts)).
- Production blob storage is unavailable unless both RPC and gateway origins validate; localnet storage is loopback-only and in-memory.
- Relay RFQ row cleanup at envelope expiry and short technical quota/lease windows ([`rfq-replay-do.ts:51-60`](../workers/relay/src/rfq-replay-do.ts#L51-L60), [`gate.ts:247-263`](../workers/relay/src/gate.ts#L247-L263)).
- `cache-control: no-store` on bootstrap/relay responses; this controls HTTP caching, not every log or persisted copy ([`bootstrap.ts:171-200`](../workers/relay/src/bootstrap.ts#L171-L200), [`ohttp.ts:59-63`](../workers/relay/src/ohttp.ts#L59-L63)).

### Not implemented or not established by code

- No accepted controller/processor allocation, privacy policy, retention schedule, legal hold rule, deletion SLA, rights-request workflow, or contact point.
- No general maximum age or automatic purge for mailbox vaults, contacts, Sent plaintext, OTC/payment records, or browser RFQ records.
- No deletion/compaction schedule for maker WAL, transcript journal, or coordinator journal.
- Portable tombstones cannot help a fresh device that is given only an older authentic snapshot from before the deletion; preserving the newest authenticated backup remains operationally necessary.
- No deployment-specific pin/unpin/retention policy for IPFS RPC/gateways, or policy for relay/platform logs, optional proxy audit sinks, observability/APM, backups, replicas, incident captures, RPC/prover/discovery providers, Privy, or CoinGecko.
- No recall or deletion of data disclosed to recipients/makers/IPFS services and no erasure of public-chain data.
- No proof that a configured deployment applies the code’s technical expiry routines, that backups follow them, or that third parties honor the same durations.

## Unanswered operator and legal questions

These require accountable operator and counsel decisions; code cannot answer them:

1. Who is controller, joint controller, processor, maker/operator, and contact point for each deployment and data flow?
2. Which jurisdictions, user/maker eligibility rules, sanctions controls, financial-services/licensing analyses, age/geographic limits, and regulator obligations apply?
3. What lawful basis and purpose applies to wallet identifiers, contact/payment correspondence, RFQ records, fraud/security metadata, support evidence, and optional audits?
4. What exact retention period and deletion trigger applies to every browser record, relay/Durable Object row, maker WAL/transcript, coordinator journal, IPFS pin/gateway cache, log, audit sink, backup, replica, RPC/prover/discovery record, Privy record, and CoinGecko request?
5. Which records require legal hold, financial/regulatory recordkeeping, fraud defense, dispute evidence, or immutable-chain disclosure, and how are conflicts with deletion requests handled?
6. How can a person access, correct, export, object, restrict, or request deletion, and how is identity verified without creating additional risk?
7. What notices, contracts, data-processing terms, international-transfer mechanisms, subprocessor inventory, incident process, and security/retention commitments are accepted with Privy, Cloudflare/platform operators, IPFS RPC/gateways, RPC, prover/discovery, makers/coordinator, CoinGecko, and observability vendors?
8. How will recipients be told that URL fragments and decrypted messages can be copied, and that public-chain facts/ciphertext cannot be erased?
9. What recovery/rotation/revocation process governs a compromised mailbox seed, local address-book key, Privy identity, maker key, or operator secret?
10. Which production configuration, logging settings, backup topology, and operator runbooks were independently verified, by whom, and when?

Until those questions are answered and an accountable operator/legal review accepts a real deployment, **the legal/data-protection release gate remains open and release-blocking**. This disclosure is engineering evidence only and must not be presented as policy acceptance.
