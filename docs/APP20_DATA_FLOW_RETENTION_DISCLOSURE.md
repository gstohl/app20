# APP20 engineering data-flow and retention disclosure

> **Engineering disclosure only — not a privacy policy, terms, legal notice, or launch acceptance.** This document describes behavior evidenced by the cited repository code. It does not identify a controller, choose a lawful basis, promise a retention period, establish a rights process, or approve any deployment. The application has no privacy/terms route ([`src/app/routes.ts:1-15`](../src/app/routes.ts#L1-L15)). **P0-34 remains open** pending an accountable operator, deployment-specific facts, and legal/regulatory/data-protection review; this document does not close it ([`APP20_RFQ_GAPS.md:128-132`](APP20_RFQ_GAPS.md#L128-L132)).

## Scope and reading rules

This is a code-derived map for engineers and reviewers. “Stored” means code deliberately writes a record; it does not imply that browser, platform, network, backup, observability, or upstream operators retain nothing else. “Expiry” means a lifecycle status or access window changes; it is **not deletion** unless the cited code removes the record. Browser deletion cannot delete copies already shared with recipients, services, backups, or a public chain.

## Public-chain facts and deletion limits

Encryption hides selected content; it does not make chain activity private or erasable. Application code discloses that on-chain Mail ciphertext, size, timing, and helper activity are public and cannot be deleted ([`contacts/page.tsx:279-286`](../src/app/contacts/page.tsx#L279-L286)); public mailbox-key registration is submitted as a helper transaction ([`Onboard.tsx:191-218`](../src/components/mail/Onboard.tsx#L191-L218)). Multi-recipient Mail additionally leaks explicit recipient count, while its ECDH-derived tags are designed not to reveal recipient identities ([`mail.ts:454-466`](../src/lib/mail.ts#L454-L466)). Shield/unshield amounts and timing remain publicly correlatable ([`funding/page.tsx:8-14`](../src/app/funding/page.tsx#L8-L14)); escrow contract events, withdrawals, OPEN-note payouts, and token amounts are public ([`EscrowCard.tsx:98-104`](../src/components/mail/EscrowCard.tsx#L98-L104)), and the preflight surfaces supplied settlement fields as publicly revealed ([`privacy-preflight.ts:353-380`](../src/lib/privacy-preflight.ts#L353-L380)). Public RPC submissions remain visible to RPC providers and Starknet.

Accordingly:

- deleting browser `localStorage`, `sessionStorage`, or IndexedDB does not delete chain ciphertext, registration, transactions, events, amounts, timing, or identifiers;
- expiry, cancellation, refusal, settlement, refund, locking a mailbox, and clearing a response cache are not global deletion;
- deleting a local contact does not retract an encrypted backup already posted;
- clearing a sender’s Sent copy does not delete a recipient’s copy;
- RFQ deletion in one browser does not delete maker, coordinator, relay, RPC, chain, log, backup, or counterparty copies; and
- no code can guarantee erasure from browser sync, device/OS backups, platform logs, RPC archives, indexers, explorers, or third-party backups.

## Implemented retention controls versus absent policy

### Implemented in code

- Explicit mailbox-profile purge for enumerated local mailbox prefixes and tab pending-payment handoff ([`local-mailbox-storage.ts:11-43`](../src/lib/local-mailbox-storage.ts#L11-L43), [`inbox/page.tsx:2200-2233`](../src/app/inbox/page.tsx#L2200-L2233)).
- Per-contact removal by encrypted-book rewrite ([`address-book.ts:279-289`](../src/lib/address-book.ts#L279-L289)).
- Pending payment handoff clear/consume ([`pending-payment.ts:39-58`](../src/lib/pending-payment.ts#L39-L58)).
- Restricted RFQ “forget” only after specified terminal, local-non-authoritative states; fail-closed tombstoning otherwise ([`rfq-lifecycle.ts:1422-1427`](../src/app/rfq/rfq-lifecycle.ts#L1422-L1427), [`rfq-storage.ts:422-457`](../src/app/rfq/rfq-storage.ts#L422-L457)).
- Relay RFQ row cleanup at envelope expiry and short technical quota/lease windows ([`rfq-replay-do.ts:51-60`](../workers/relay/src/rfq-replay-do.ts#L51-L60), [`gate.ts:247-263`](../workers/relay/src/gate.ts#L247-L263)).
- `cache-control: no-store` on bootstrap/relay responses; this controls HTTP caching, not every log or persisted copy ([`bootstrap.ts:171-200`](../workers/relay/src/bootstrap.ts#L171-L200), [`ohttp.ts:59-63`](../workers/relay/src/ohttp.ts#L59-L63)).

### Not implemented or not established by code

- No accepted controller/processor allocation, privacy policy, retention schedule, legal hold rule, deletion SLA, rights-request workflow, or contact point.
- No general maximum age or automatic purge for mailbox vaults, contacts, Sent plaintext, OTC/payment records, or browser RFQ records.
- No deletion/compaction schedule for maker WAL or coordinator journal.
- No deployment-specific policy for relay/platform logs, optional proxy audit sinks, observability/APM, backups, replicas, incident captures, RPC providers, prover/discovery gateways, Privy, or CoinGecko.
- No recall or deletion of data disclosed to recipients/makers and no erasure of public-chain data.
- No proof that a configured deployment applies the code’s technical expiry routines, that backups follow them, or that third parties honor the same durations.

## Unanswered operator and legal questions (P0-34)

These require accountable operator and counsel decisions; code cannot answer them:

1. Who is controller, joint controller, processor, maker/operator, and contact point for each deployment and data flow?
2. Which jurisdictions, user/maker eligibility rules, sanctions controls, financial-services/licensing analyses, age/geographic limits, and regulator obligations apply?
3. What lawful basis and purpose applies to wallet identifiers, contact/payment correspondence, RFQ records, fraud/security metadata, support evidence, and optional audits?
4. What exact retention period and deletion trigger applies to every browser record, relay/Durable Object row, maker WAL, coordinator journal, log, audit sink, backup, replica, RPC/prover/discovery record, Privy record, and CoinGecko request?
5. Which records require legal hold, financial/regulatory recordkeeping, fraud defense, dispute evidence, or immutable-chain disclosure, and how are conflicts with deletion requests handled?
6. How can a person access, correct, export, object, restrict, or request deletion, and how is identity verified without creating additional risk?
7. What notices, contracts, data-processing terms, international-transfer mechanisms, subprocessor inventory, incident process, and security/retention commitments are accepted with Privy, Cloudflare/platform operators, RPC, prover/discovery, makers/coordinator, CoinGecko, and observability vendors?
8. How will recipients be told that URL fragments and decrypted messages can be copied, and that public-chain facts/ciphertext cannot be erased?
9. What recovery/rotation/revocation process governs a compromised mailbox seed, local address-book key, Privy identity, maker key, or operator secret?
10. Which production configuration, logging settings, backup topology, and operator runbooks were independently verified, by whom, and when?

Until those questions are answered and an accountable operator/legal review accepts a real deployment, **P0-34 remains open and release-blocking**. This disclosure is engineering evidence only and must not be presented as policy acceptance.
